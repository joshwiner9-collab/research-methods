How to use this

A learn day takes about 45 minutes. It opens with two recall questions on an older day and one guess about today. Then three panels, each moving one step up: the idea in plain words, the rule you must be able to apply, a worked example from the course notes one operation at a time, and two try-its of your own. It ends with a quiz where every answer comes with a full worked explanation, including why each wrong option tempts.

A mock day takes about three hours: the paper, then going through what you missed. Every question you get wrong is saved to your error log, in this browser. On the sweep day and under each mock, a button turns the log into a prompt for Claude, so it drills you on exactly what you keep missing.

The log lives only in this browser, on this device. Clearing your browsing data erases it. The prompt text doubles as a backup.

No phones, laptops, tablets, smart watches, or anything that can communicate or store files. A t-table is attached to the paper. The practice papers follow a fixed pattern: questions 1 to 13 easy, 14 to 18 intermediate, 19 difficult, 20 very hard. The final is worth 80% of the grade, next to homework (10%), participation (5%) and quizzes (5%).

Where this came from

Built from your course files: the slides for all seven lessons, the class exercises for sessions 1 to 3, class assignments 4, 5, 6A, 6B and 7, and the two practice exams with their solutions. Worked examples use the professor's own examples wherever the notes have one: the bread study, the families, the quantum course, Dr. Havatzelet's coffee clinic, Daniela's raise, the coffee chain, the class drills and the CAPM printout.

Mocks 1 and 2 are the professor's own practice papers, questions 1 to 18. Questions 19 and 20 were not in the files, so those papers stop at 18. Mock 3 and the learn-day quiz questions were written for this plan, in the same style. None of it is a real past final. Every number was worked out again in code.

Where two files disagree, the page says so. The one that matters: two-sample degrees of freedom. See day 6.
