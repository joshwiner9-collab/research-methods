The centre: mode, median, mean

Three honest answers to "what is typical?". They disagree for a reason, and the reason is the shape of the data.

Five minutes: recall

1. Which measures of centre are legal for an ordinal variable such as satisfaction 1 to 5?

2. "Does ad spend drive monthly sales?" Which variable is dependent?

Say both answers out loud before you press the button.

1. The mode and the median. There is an order, so a middle value exists. The mean assumes equal gaps, which the scale does not promise.

2. Monthly sales. It is the thing being explained. Ad spend is the independent variable.

Guess first

Five salaries: 3,000; 4,000; 4,700; 5,000; 5,500. One student then lands a tech job and the 5,500 becomes 55,000. Which moves more: the mean or the median? Roughly by how much?

This is a guess, not a test. No answer is shown. You will know by the end of the page.

1. Three definitions of typical

Mode: the most common value. It works for every type of variable, even nominal. It can be missing (all values equally common) or plural: the families data 2, 2, 6, 5, 3, 5, 5, 4, 3, 2 has two modes, 2 and 5.

Median: the middle of the sorted data. Its position is (n + 1)/2. With an even n there are two middle seats, and you average them. The formula gives a seat number, not a value: find the seat, then read who sits there.

Mean: the sum divided by n, Σx / n. It is the balance point: the deviations from it always sum to zero, Σ(x − x̄) = 0. That fact comes back on day 4 and day 5.

You will need the Σ sign. It respects plus, minus and constants: Σa = n·a, Σa·x = a·Σx, Σ(x + y) = Σx + Σy. It does not respect products or powers: Σxy ≠ Σx·Σy and Σx² ≠ (Σx)². Expand first, then sum term by term.

The mean feels every value, so one extreme value drags it. The median only cares about order, so it barely moves. Under extreme values, quote the median as "typical".

Worked example

Five salaries: 3,000; 4,000; 4,700; 5,000; 5,500. The 5,500 becomes 55,000. Compute the mean and median before and after.
1
Mean before.
(3,000 + 4,000 + 4,700 + 5,000 + 5,500) / 5 = 22,200 / 5 = 4,440
2
Median before.
n = 5, so seat (5 + 1)/2 = 3. The third sorted value is 4,700.
3
Mean after.
(3,000 + 4,000 + 4,700 + 5,000 + 55,000) / 5 = 71,700 / 5 = 14,340
4
Median after.
Still sorted, still 5 values. Seat 3 still holds 4,700.
5
Read it.
The mean more than tripled and now describes nobody: four people earn far less, one far more. The median did not move.
Answer: the mean goes from 4,440 to 14,340; the median stays 4,700.
Try it. Scores 50, 60, 60, 70, 80, 90. What is the median?
n = 6 is even, so seats 3 and 4: (60 + 70) / 2 = 65. Nobody scored 65, and that is fine for an even n.
Try it. x = 1, 2, 3 and y = 3, 4, 6. Compute Σ(2x + 3y) using the rules.
Σ(2x + 3y) = 2Σx + 3Σy = 2·6 + 3·13 = 12 + 39 = 51.

2. The centre from a table, and from classes

From a frequency table, each value votes as many times as it appears: mean = Σx·f / n. The median: walk down the F column until it first reaches the middle seat.

Combining groups: weight each group mean by its size. 30 calls averaging 6 minutes and 20 averaging 11 give (30·6 + 20·11) / 50 = 8, not 8.5.

From classes, we no longer know the exact values. For the mean, each case stands at its class midpoint. For the mode, take the class with the highest density and report its midpoint. For the median, the slides report the median class, the class holding seat n/2. The assignments and practice exams go one step further and walk into that class:

Median = L + ((n/2 − F before the class) / f of the class) × width
For a grouped median, first find the class where F first reaches n/2. Then interpolate inside it. The class midpoint is the trap answer.

Worked example

Scores of 100 students: 40–60: 5, 60–70: 10, 70–75: 20, 75–85: 40, 85–100: 25. Find the median class, then estimate the median.
1
Build F.
F = 5, 15, 35, 75, 100
2
Find seat n/2.
n/2 = 50. F(70–75) = 35 < 50 ≤ 75 = F(75–85)
3
Name the median class.
75–85. This is where the slides stop.
4
Walk into the class.
L = 75, F before = 35, f = 40, width = 10
5
Interpolate.
75 + (50 − 35) / 40 × 10 = 75 + 3.75 = 78.75
Answer: the median class is 75–85, and the median is about 78.75. The midpoint trap would give 80.
Try it. The quantum scores: 40–60: 5, 60–70: 5, 70–75: 10, 75–85: 10, 85–100: 10. Estimate the mean with midpoints.
Midpoints 50, 65, 72.5, 80, 92.5. Σm·f = 250 + 325 + 725 + 800 + 925 = 3,025. Mean = 3,025 / 40 = 75.6.
Try it. Same quantum table. What is the mode?
Three classes tie on f = 10, so density breaks the tie: 70–75 has d = 2. The mode is its midpoint, 72.5.

3. Shape decides who wins

In a symmetric bell, mode, median and mean sit together at the peak. Skew pulls them apart in a fixed order. The mean chases the tail, the median follows halfway, the mode stays at the peak.

Right (positive) skew: mode < median < mean. Wages, house prices and customer spend look like this.
ShapeOrderExamples
Symmetricmode = median = meanheights
Right (positive) skewmode < median < meanwages, prices, spend
Left (negative) skewmean < median < modeeasy-exam scores, retirement age

Two more averages from the Lesson 4 warm-up. Average speed over equal distances is the harmonic mean: 100 km/h there and 50 km/h back is 200 km in 3 hours, 66.7 km/h, not 75. Growth rates compound, so they need the geometric mean.

Mean above median means a right tail. Mean below median means a left tail. Under a right tail, more than half the values sit below the mean.

Worked example

An exam question from the slides: "Graduate wages are right-skewed. So the share of graduates earning more than the average wage is greater than the share earning less." True or false?
1
Translate the shape.
Right skew: a few very high wages stretch the right tail.
2
Place the mean.
The mean chases the tail, so it sits to the right of the median.
3
Use what the median means.
Half of all graduates earn at or below the median.
4
Combine.
Everyone below the median is also below the mean, plus everyone between the two. So more than half earn below the mean.
Answer: FALSE. The claim is backwards.
Try it. "Scores on an easy exam are left-skewed, so more than half the students scored above the class average." True or false?
TRUE. Left skew drags the mean down, below the median. Half the class is above the median, which is already above the mean, plus everyone between.
Try it. A stock goes +100%, then −50%, then −50%. What is the average daily return that compounds correctly?
Growth factors 2.0 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.5. Geometric mean: 0.51/3 = 0.794, so −20.6% a day. The arithmetic mean says 0%, but you lost half your money.
The one thing to carry into tomorrow
  • Median seat (n + 1)/2 of sorted data; the mean is the balance point.
  • Weight group means by group size.
  • The mean chases the tail.
