The spread: how far apart are the values?

Two classes can share the same average and be nothing alike. Today: four rulers for spread, from the crudest to the one the exam loves.

Five minutes: recall

1. A class 10 to 30 holds 40 cases and a class 30 to 35 holds 15. Which is more crowded?

2. 100 values: F(3) = 38 and F(4) = 65. How many values are at least 4?

Say both answers out loud before you press the button.

1. 30 to 35. Density 15 / 5 = 3 against 40 / 20 = 2. Crowding is density, not count.

2. n − F(3) = 100 − 38 = 62. "At least 4" subtracts everything up to the value just below 4.

Guess first

Two sections both average 75. In section A every score is between 71 and 79. In section B half the class scored in the 50s and half in the 90s. What single number would tell the two sections apart?

This is a guess, not a test. No answer is shown. You will know by the end of the page.

1. Percentiles and quartiles: where do you rank?

A percentile Pz is the value with z% of the data at or below it. A few have their own names: Q1 = P25, the median = P50, Q3 = P75, and the first decile = P10. The fifth decile is the median.

On a frequency table the slides use a "dial": dial = n × z / 100. Walk down F, and the first value whose F reaches the dial is the answer. The dial is a seat number, never the answer itself.

On a short raw list, the assignments use positions: Q1 at (n + 1)/4, Q3 at 3(n + 1)/4, on the sorted data.

Sort first. Find the seat (dial or position). Then read the value that sits there.

Dial = 60. F first reaches 60 at x = 3 (F = 65). P30 = 3 cups.

2. Four rulers for spread

Each ruler answers "how spread out?" in a different way.

RulerFormulaStrengthWeakness
Rangemax − minone subtractionhostage to the two most extreme values
IQRQ3 − Q1ignores outliers; spread of the middle halfignores half the data
Variance s²Σ(x − x̄)² / nuses every valueunits are squared: cups², shekels²
Standard deviation s√s²uses every value, back in real unitsoutliers pull it

Why square the deviations? Because plain deviations always sum to zero (the mean is the balance point), so their average is always zero and tells you nothing. Squaring makes every distance count as positive.

This course always divides by n (Excel VAR.P and STDEV.P). A shortcut that gives the same answer: s² = Σx²/n − x̄².

Standard deviation = the typical distance of a value from the mean, in the variable's own units. Divide by n.

Worked example

Seven grades: 89, 71, 88, 83, 61, 77, 91. Find the variance and the standard deviation.
1
Mean.
Σx = 560 → x̄ = 560 / 7 = 80
2
Deviations d = x − 80.
+9, −9, +8, +3, −19, −3, +11  (sum = 0 ✓)
3
Square them.
81, 81, 64, 9, 361, 9, 121 → Σd² = 726
4
Divide by n.
s² = 726 / 7 = 103.71 (points²)
5
Take the root.
s = √103.71 = 10.18 points
6
Read it.
A typical grade sits about 10 points from 80. The 61 alone contributes 361 of the 726, which is why s hears outliers.
Answer: s² = 103.71, s = 10.18 points.
Try it. Data 9, 4, 1, 12, 19. Find s² and s.
Mean = 45 / 5 = 9. d = 0, −5, −8, +3, +10. Σd² = 0 + 25 + 64 + 9 + 100 = 198. s² = 198 / 5 = 39.6, s = 6.29.
Try it. The range of a dataset is 0. What is its IQR? And if the IQR is 0, what is the range?
Range 0 means every value is the same, so the IQR is 0 too. But IQR 0 only fixes the middle half; the extremes are free, so the range can be anything.

3. Changing the data, and reading the shape from quartiles

Suppose every value is changed the same way: y = a + b·x. Adding a moves everything together. Multiplying by b stretches every distance.

ChangeMeanss²
Add a to every value+ ano changeno change
Multiply every value by b× b× |b|× b²
Both: y = a + b·xa + b·x̄|b|·sb²·s²

The quartiles also show the shape, without being fooled by outliers. Symmetric: the gap Q3 − Q2 equals the gap Q2 − Q1. A wider upper gap means right skew; a wider lower gap means left skew.

Adding a constant never changes spread. Multiplying scales s by |b| and the variance by b². Square the factor for the variance.

Worked example

The seven grades (mean 80, s² 103.71, s 10.18) are raised by 5%. Find the new mean, variance and standard deviation without recomputing the table.
1
Name the change.
A 5% raise is y = 1.05·x, so b = 1.05 and a = 0.
2
Mean.
1.05 × 80 = 84
3
Standard deviation.
1.05 × 10.18 = 10.69
4
Variance: square the factor.
1.05² × 103.71 = 1.1025 × 103.71 = 114.35
5
Check.
10.69² = 114.3 ✓
Answer: mean 84, s = 10.69, s² = 114.35.
Try it. Instead, every student gets a 2-point bonus. New mean and s?
Mean 80 + 2 = 82. s stays 10.18: everyone moved together, so no distance changed.
Try it. Five-number summary of the coffee data: min 0, Q1 3, median 4, Q3 5, max 7. Is the middle half symmetric?
Q2 − Q1 = 1 and Q3 − Q2 = 1. The gaps are equal, so the middle half is symmetric.
The one thing to carry into tomorrow
  • Percentiles: sort, find the seat, read the value.
  • Divide by n. s is the typical distance from the mean.
  • +a leaves spread alone; ×b scales s by |b| and s² by b².
