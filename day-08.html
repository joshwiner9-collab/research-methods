The line: regression

r says how tightly two things move together. The regression line says by how much, and lets you predict. Today you fit one by hand and read a software printout.

2. What is a Type II error?

1. n₁ + n₂ − 2 = 25.

2. Not rejecting H0 when it is actually false: missing a real effect.

Guess first

Four points: x = 200, 250, 300, 350 and y = 180, 230, 280, 310. Without any formula, roughly what y would you predict at x = 280?

This is a guess, not a test. No answer is shown. You will know by the end of the page.

1. The model and least squares

Regression writes the data as a model plus an error. Greek letters are the unknown truth; letters with a hat are what the sample estimates.

yᵢ = β₀ + β₁·xᵢ + εᵢ   ·   fitted line: ŷ = β̂₀ + β̂₁·x

The residual e = y − ŷ is data minus model, never the other way round. Many lines could pass through a cloud of dots. Least squares picks the one with the smallest Σe². Squares, for the same reason as the variance: plain misses above and below cancel.

The least-squares line has two fixed habits. It always passes through the point of means (x̄, ȳ). Its residuals always sum to zero.

β̂₁ = Σdₓ·dᵧ / Σdₓ² = (n·Σxy − Σx·Σy) / (n·Σx² − (Σx)²)  ·  β̂₀ = ȳ − β̂₁·x̄

The slope is the covariance divided by the variance of x. Written another way, β̂₁ = r · sᵧ / sₓ, so the slope always has r's sign.

Slope first, by either route; then the intercept from the means. Residual = data minus model.

Worked example

The slides' drill: x = 200, 250, 300, 350 and y = 180, 230, 280, 310. Fit the least-squares line.
1
The sums.
n = 4 · Σx = 1,100 · Σy = 1,000 · Σxy = 286,000 · Σx² = 315,000
2
Top of the slope.
n·Σxy − Σx·Σy = 4 × 286,000 − 1,100 × 1,000 = 1,144,000 − 1,100,000 = 44,000
3
Bottom of the slope.
n·Σx² − (Σx)² = 4 × 315,000 − 1,100² = 1,260,000 − 1,210,000 = 50,000
4
Slope.
β̂₁ = 44,000 / 50,000 = 0.88
5
Intercept, from the means.
x̄ = 275, ȳ = 250 → β̂₀ = 250 − 0.88 × 275 = 250 − 242 = 8
6
Check: through the means.
8 + 0.88 × 275 = 250 = ȳ ✓
Answer: ŷ = 8 + 0.88·x.
The fitted line with each residual in red. Two points sit above the line, two below; the misses sum to zero.
Try it. Using ŷ = 8 + 0.88x, predict y at x = 280. How close was your guess from the top of the page?
ŷ = 8 + 0.88 × 280 = 254.4.
Try it. Find the four residuals and check that they sum to zero.
ŷ = 184, 228, 272, 316. e = 180 − 184 = −4; 230 − 228 = +2; 280 − 272 = +8; 310 − 316 = −6. Sum: −4 + 2 + 8 − 6 = 0 ✓.

2. Reading a line, and R²

Slope: per one extra unit of x, ŷ changes by β̂₁ on average. Intercept: ŷ when x = 0. It only means something if x = 0 is inside the data. Predicting outside the range of x is extrapolation, and the line gives no support for it. And a line shows moving together, not cause.

R² is the share of the variation in y that the line explains. Compare the line with the laziest forecast, a flat line at ȳ:

R² = 1 − Σe² / Σ(y − ȳ)²   ·   in simple regression R² = r²

R² is never negative. To go back: r = ±√R², with the slope's sign. R² = 0.75 does not mean "right 75% of the time"; it means three quarters of the ups and downs in y line up with x. In messy human data 0.10 can be a real finding; 0.99 usually means a textbook, or a mistake.

Interpret the slope "per one unit of x, on average". Predict only inside the data. R² = r², and r takes the slope's sign.

Worked example

The slides' ad budget case: ads x = 1 to 5, sales y = 4, 7, 7, 10, 12 (₪100K). Sxy = 19, Sxx = 10, Σ(y − ȳ)² = 38. Fit the line, find r and R², and read the slope.
1
Slope.
β̂₁ = Sxy / Sxx = 19 / 10 = 1.9
2
Intercept.
x̄ = 3, ȳ = 8 → β̂₀ = 8 − 1.9 × 3 = 2.3
3
r.
19 / √(10 × 38) = 19 / 19.49 = 0.975
4
R².
0.975² = 0.95
5
Read it.
Each extra ₪100K of ads goes with ₪190K more sales, on average. Ads line up with 95% of the variation in sales in these five months.
Answer: ŷ = 2.3 + 1.9x, r = 0.975, R² = 0.95.
Try it. The slides' salary line is ŷ = 7,657 + 494.6x (x = years of experience). A 12-year employee earns 13,000. Find the residual.
ŷ = 7,657 + 494.6 × 12 = 13,592. e = 13,000 − 13,592 = −592: she earns 592 less than the line predicts.
Try it. r = 0.5, sᵧ = 10 and sₓ = 4. What is the slope?
β̂₁ = r · sᵧ / sₓ = 0.5 × 10 / 4 = 1.25.

3. The software printout, and CAPM

Software prints each coefficient with a standard error, a t and a p-value. The printed t always tests "coefficient = 0". To test any other value, compute your own:

t = (coefficient − value tested) / SE   ·   df = n − 2

The verdict works as on day 6: compare |t| with the table, or reject if p < α.

In finance, CAPM regresses a stock's excess return on the market's: (R − r_f) = α + β·(R_m − r_f) + e. β is the slope, the market sensitivity: above 1 is aggressive, below 1 defensive. α is the intercept, the return the market does not explain. For a stock, "β = 0" is almost never the interesting question; "β = 1" is.

The printed t tests "= 0". For β = 1, compute (β̂ − 1) / SE yourself, with df = n − 2.

Worked example

A printout for 60 months: β = 1.35 (SE 0.15, t 9.00, p < 0.001), α = 0.40 (SE 0.50, t 0.80, p 0.43), R² = 0.58. Is the stock riskier than the market, and does the manager have "alpha"?
1
Test β = 1.
t = (1.35 − 1) / 0.15 = 0.35 / 0.15 = 2.33
2
Read the table.
df = 58, not printed → row 40, two-tail 0.05: 2.021 → 2.33 > 2.021 → reject
3
Test α = 0.
t = 0.40 / 0.50 = 0.80 < 2.021 → do not reject (p = 0.43 agrees)
4
Read R².
r = √0.58 = 0.76. The market explains 58%; the other 42% is the stock's own news.
5
Conclude.
β is significantly above 1: the stock amplifies market swings. The 0.4% monthly alpha is well within noise: a number, not a finding.
Answer: riskier than the market (β > 1 is significant); no evidence of alpha.
Try it. Same stock. Predict its excess return in a month when the market's is +2%, and when it is −2%.
ŷ = 0.40 + 1.35 × 2 = +3.1%. ŷ = 0.40 − 2.70 = −2.3%. Aggressive both ways.
Try it. Why is the printout's own t = 9.00 for β almost useless to a risk manager?
It tests β = 0: "the stock ignores the market", which is absurd for almost any stock. The useful question is β = 1, and you have to compute that t yourself.
The one thing to carry into tomorrow
  • β̂₁ = Σdₓdᵧ / Σdₓ²; β̂₀ = ȳ − β̂₁x̄; the line passes through the means.
  • e = y − ŷ. R² = r², r takes the slope's sign.
  • Printed t tests 0. Anything else: (b − value) / SE, df n − 2.
